Why do I still write on paper?

HomeWritingEssay
Elaheh MehrayinSeptember 30, 20266 min read

Every morning, before I turn on my phone, I write three pages in my notebook with a pen. Most of the time it's something rambling: a dream I had, the sound of the rain that fell in the night, or a sentence that has stayed in my mind since yesterday. My friends ask why, in an age when you can type hundreds of words with a few keystrokes, I'm still loyal to paper.

Paper is slower

The hand moves more slowly than thought, and that slowness is a gift. When I write with a pen, I have to stay with each sentence a little longer before writing it down. Thoughts have time to settle, and what reaches the paper is more honest.

“Writing on paper is like walking: you may arrive later, but you see everything along the way.”— from the author's notebooks

Paper has a memory

In computer files, earlier versions are erased. But in my notebooks the crossings-out remain. Years later, when I go back to them, I can see which word I set aside and why. Half the characters of “The Last Train North” were born from those crossings-out.

  • Paper sends no notifications and doesn't distract me.
  • On paper you can draw lines and arrows and write in the margins.
  • Notebooks are a constant companion: on the train, in a café, in the bakery queue.

So what about the keyboard?

It wouldn't be fair to say I don't write on a computer. The second and third drafts always take shape on screen, where editing is easy and I can see the structure better. But the first spark is almost always struck on paper.

If you are a writer too, or would like to write, here is my suggestion: for one week, write three pages by hand every morning. Don't judge, just write. Perhaps, like me, you'll hear a new voice of your own.
