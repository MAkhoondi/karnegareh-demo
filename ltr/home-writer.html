From the book

Chapter one: Eleven at night

That night Tehran railway station smelled of rain and stewed tea. Mahtab held her suitcase tighter and looked at the green board where the names of cities lit up and went dark one by one. For years she had run from this station, and now, just when she thought there was nothing left to go back for, a one-way ticket sat in her pocket.

Carriage four was still empty. She took the window seat and closed her eyes as soon as the train moved. She did not know that the six people who would step into the compartment one by one each had a reason not to arrive tonight.

About me

A life among books

Born in 1985 in Rasht. MA in Persian literature from the University of Tehran. I published my first story at 19 in Hamshahri Dastan magazine, and writing has never let me go since.

My work has been translated into English, Turkish and Italian. Alongside writing, I run fiction workshops and write a weekly column for Kargadan.
